# Research notes, causes from outside the dataset

Requirement 3 asks for causes drawn from outside the data. Every external claim in the
report cites a note here. Requirement 4 claims are kept out of this file, because those
must be argued from inside the data.

Each note states what the data shows first, then the outside evidence, then the limit of
the claim.

---

## N1. The 2020 COVID demand collapse

**What the data shows.** Main category 3 export falls 37,467 million dollars in 2020, a
fall of 30%. Petroleum import falls 41% and petroleum export falls 37%. The manufacturing
categories are the statistical outliers of the year: category 8 at z = 2.41, category 6
at z = 2.37 and category 7 at z = 2.15.

**Outside evidence.** World energy demand fell sharply through 2020 as movement
restrictions cut transport fuel use. Australian resource and energy export earnings fell
with world prices before recovering from 2021.

**Source.** Department of Industry, Science and Resources, Resources and Energy Quarterly,
March 2022. https://www.industry.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-09/resources-and-energy-quarterly-march-2022.pdf

**Limit of the claim.** The dataset holds values in dollars, not volumes. It cannot
separate a price fall from a volume fall. The report must say "value" and not "demand"
when it quotes these numbers.

## N2. The 2022 energy price shock

**What the data shows.** Main category 3 export rises 124,939 million dollars in 2022, a
rise of 99%, which is the largest single year move in the dataset. Coal export rises 124%
and gas export rises 79%. Petroleum import rises 91%. Category 3 records z = 2.15, the
outlier of the year.

**Outside evidence.** Gas, LNG and coal prices passed previous record highs in 2022 after
Russia's invasion of Ukraine, as northern hemisphere buyers replaced Russian supply.
Australian resource and energy exports set a record of 464 billion dollars in 2022 to
2023. Thermal coal export earnings reached 65 billion dollars that financial year, up from
about 16 billion in 2020 to 2021. LNG earnings were forecast at 91 billion dollars, three
times the 2020 to 2021 figure.

**Source.** Department of Industry, Science and Resources, media release, "Resources
earnings normalise after Ukraine war price spike".
https://www.minister.industry.gov.au/king/media/resources-earnings-normalise-after-ukraine-war-price-spike

**Limit of the claim.** The dataset is on calendar years and the department reports on
financial years, so the figures are close but not directly comparable.

## N3. The unwind after 2022

**What the data shows.** Main category 3 export falls three years in a row: 22% in 2023,
13% in 2024 and 18% in 2025. Coal export falls 27%, 17% then 25%.

**Outside evidence.** The 2022 record prices did not persist. Combined thermal and
metallurgical coal export earnings were forecast to fall from 90 billion dollars in 2023
to 2024 to 70 billion by 2025 to 2026. LNG export revenue was forecast to fall from 69
billion to 59 billion over the same period, because world prices eased from the 2022
highs.

**Source.** As N2.

**Limit of the claim.** This is a price normalisation, not a loss of market. The data
cannot show volumes, so the report must not call this a fall in production.

## N4. Australian refinery closures and petroleum import reliance

**What the data shows.** Petroleum import rises from 23,315 million dollars in 2016 to
52,742 million in 2025, a rise of 126%. The petroleum trade deficit widens from 16,369
million to 38,555 million. Petroleum is the only large subcategory where Australia buys
far more than it sells, at a cover ratio of 0.27 in 2025.

**Outside evidence.** BP announced it would stop its 146,000 barrel a day Kwinana
refinery in Western Australia by the end of March 2021, citing regional oversupply and
sustained low refining margins. In February 2021 ExxonMobil decided to close its 90,000
barrel a day Altona refinery in Victoria. The Altona closure left only two Australian
refineries running, Geelong and Lytton, with combined capacity under a quarter of the
Australian market of about one million barrels a day. Viva Energy committed to keep
refining at Geelong to June 2027 under the government Fuel Security Package.

**Sources.**
- Enerdata, "BP will stop its Kwinana refinery in Australia by end-March 2021".
  https://www.enerdata.net/publications/daily-energy-news/bp-will-stop-its-kwinana-refinery-australia-end-march-2021.html
- Argus Media, "ExxonMobil Australia to shut 90,000 b/d Altona refinery".
  https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2185554-exxonmobil-australia-to-shut-90-000-b-d-altona-refinery
- Argus Media, "Australia refinery closures raise fuel import reliance".
  https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2185574-australia-refinery-closures-raise-fuel-import-reliance
- Parliamentary Library, "Australian oil refineries and fuel security", December 2020.
  https://www.aph.gov.au/About_Parliament/Parliamentary_departments/Parliamentary_Library/Research/FlagPost/2020/December/Oil_refineries_and_fuel_security

**Limit of the claim.** Both closures fall in 2021, and the import value keeps rising
after it. The closures change the mix of imports from crude towards refined product. They
are not the cause of the 2022 jump, which is a price event. The report must keep these two
causes apart.

## N5. China's restrictions on Australian coal

**What the data shows.** Coal export rises 124% in 2022, in the middle of the restriction
period, and then falls for three years after the restrictions end.

**Outside evidence.** China imposed unofficial restrictions on Australian coal from late
2020. Four state backed importers were allowed to resume buying in early January 2023,
and all domestic companies were allowed to import Australian coal from March 2023. China
imported 52.47 million tonnes of Australian coal in 2023, against 2.86 million tonnes in
2022.

**Sources.**
- S&P Global, "China nears lifting over two year old unofficial ban on Australian coal imports".
  https://spglobal.com/commodityinsights/en/market-insights/latest-news/coal/010423-china-nears-lifting-over-two-year-old-unofficial-ban-on-australian-coal-imports
- Mining Technology, "China lifts remaining Australia coal restrictions".
  https://www.mining-technology.com/news/china-lifts-australia-coal-ban/

**Limit of the claim, and a warning.** The restrictions did not cut Australian coal export
value. Value rose 124% during the restriction period and fell after the restrictions were
removed. Price, not market access, drove the value. Australia redirected volume to other
buyers. The report must not claim the ban reduced coal exports, because the data shows the
opposite.

## N6. Iron ore, 2019 and the 2021 peak

**What the data shows.** Crude materials export rises 29,536 million dollars in 2019, and
iron ore alone rises 32,099 million. Iron ore export peaks in 2021 at 185,259 million and
then falls 15% in 2022, in the same year that coal rises 124%. Over the decade the
correlation between coal and iron ore growth is only -0.28, and between main categories 3
and 2 it is +0.03.

**Outside evidence.** Iron ore rose through the first half of 2019 to about 126 US dollars
a tonne in July, after the Vale Brumadinho dam failure cut Brazilian supply, and Vale
volumes fell 20%. Prices then reached a record 193.85 US dollars a dry tonne on 27 April
2021, and fell to a 2021 low of 87.20 US dollars on 18 November 2021.

**Sources.**
- S&P Global, "Vale reports record Q1 as iron ore, copper prices soar in tight market".
  https://www.spglobal.com/commodity-insights/en/news-research/latest-news/metals/042721-vale-reports-record-q1-as-iron-ore-copper-prices-soar-in-tight-market
- S&P Global, "Analysis: Iron ore disruptions in southern Brazil may support recovery in spot prices".
  https://www.spglobal.com/commodity-insights/en/news-research/latest-news/metals/011022-analysis-iron-ore-disruptions-in-southern-brazil-may-support-recovery-in-spot-prices
- Mining Technology, "Iron ore wars: the fall of Vale and the rise of Rio Tinto".
  https://www.mining-technology.com/features/iron-ore-wars-the-fall-of-vale-and-the-rise-of-rio-tinto/

**Why this matters for 2022.** The iron ore price had already fallen hard by November
2021, before energy prices spiked after February 2022. So the 2022 divergence is two
separate price cycles that happen to cross, not one commodity substituting for another.
The report must present the 2022 divergence that way, and must not claim a standing
inverse relationship, because r = -0.28 over the decade does not support one.

## N7. Gold and the 2025 rise in category 9

**What the data shows.** Category 9, Commodities and transactions not classified
elsewhere, rises 21,245 million dollars in 2025 and records z = 2.52, the outlier of that
year. Subcategory 97, Gold, non-monetary, rises 23,854 million in the same year, more than
the whole category, because confidential items fall 3,006 million. Gold export grows from
18,857 million in 2016 to 59,557 million in 2025.

**Outside evidence.** Gold rallied 44% over 2025 and set 56 record highs, reaching about
4,550 US dollars an ounce in December 2025. This was the strongest year for gold since
1980. Central banks bought 863 tonnes over the year, below the recent peak above 1,000
tonnes but well above the 2010 to 2021 average of 473 tonnes. Geopolitical uncertainty and
a central bank shift away from the dollar drove the move.

**Sources.**
- World Gold Council, Gold Demand Trends, full year 2025, central banks.
  https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-demand-trends/gold-demand-trends-full-year-2025/central-banks
- Euronews, "Gold surges to record high as central banks turn from dollar to bullion".
  https://www.euronews.com/business/2025/09/02/gold-surges-to-record-high-as-central-banks-turn-from-dollar-to-bullion

**Limit of the claim.** Australian gold export value rose 67% in 2025, from 35,703 million
to 59,557 million dollars, while the gold price rose 44%. Price explains most of the rise
but not all of it. The report must not attribute the whole rise to price, because the gap
of about 23 percentage points points to higher volume or higher refining throughput as
well, and the dataset cannot separate the two.
